The Challenge

A single conveyor motor could take down the whole line

The bottler's filling line ran on a single continuous conveyor path, which meant a bearing failure anywhere along it could stop the entire line — not just one station. Historically, these failures were only caught by ear: a technician noticing an unusual sound during a walk-through, often after the wear had already progressed to the point of imminent failure.

With three shifts running and no dedicated night-shift maintenance technician, wear on the conveyor's drive motor had gone undetected for weeks before a near-miss stoppage prompted the plant to look for a better way to monitor line health.

The Solution

A focused deployment on the highest-risk asset

Rather than mirroring the entire filling line at once, Synchronix started with the conveyor's drive motor and gearbox — the single point of failure most likely to stop the whole line — and expanded sensor coverage from there.

  • → Vibration and thermal sensors added to the drive motor and both gearbox bearings
  • → Baseline model trained against two weeks of normal-operation data before going live
  • → Alerts routed to the maintenance team's existing paging system, no new software to check

"The model caught a wear pattern none of us would have noticed for another week. We fixed it on a Tuesday afternoon instead of during a Saturday night emergency call."

Reliability Engineer — Beverage Bottling Facility, Southeast US
The Results

A scheduled repair instead of an emergency one

Three weeks after going live, the twin flagged a slow but steady rise in bearing vibration on the drive motor — a pattern consistent with early-stage wear, days before it would have produced any audible symptom. The team scheduled a replacement during a planned changeover window, avoiding what modeling suggested would otherwise have been a multi-hour unplanned stoppage.
