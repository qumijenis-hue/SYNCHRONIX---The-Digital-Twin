The Challenge

Every new product configuration meant a live trial run

Launching a new package size or material meant reconfiguring the line's fill heads, sealers, and labelers — then running a live trial batch to see if the settings actually worked. A bad trial meant scrapped material, a delayed launch, and hours of line time that could have gone to production.

With customers requesting more frequent packaging variants, the engineering team was running trial batches nearly every week, and each one tied up a full line for half a shift just to validate settings that often needed a second or third attempt.

The Solution

Testing changeovers against the twin first

Synchronix mirrored the fill, seal, and label stations on both changeover-heavy lines, letting engineers simulate a new configuration's settings against the twin before committing any physical material to a trial run.

  • → New product specs entered directly into the simulation interface
  • → Twin flags likely misalignment or seal-integrity issues before the line ever moves
  • → Only configurations that pass simulation proceed to a physical trial run

"We used to find out a fill head was misaligned after wasting forty cases of product. Now we find out before we've touched a single unit."

Packaging Engineering Manager — Consumer Packaging Manufacturer, Southeast US
The Results

Faster launches, less scrapped material

Within two months, engineers were validating most new configurations entirely in simulation, reserving live trial runs for final confirmation only. Changeover testing time dropped sharply, and the line ran more product launches per month as a direct result.
