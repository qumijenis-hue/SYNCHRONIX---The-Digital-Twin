The Challenge

A plant-wide system needed proof before it got a plant-wide budget

Leadership was interested in a full digital twin rollout across the fabrication floor, but wasn't willing to commit capital for 14 lines' worth of sensors and modeling without evidence it would actually pay off. Fabrication equipment also varies more than a typical assembly line — cutting, bending, and welding stations each have very different failure modes, making a single one-size-fits-all model risky to trust plant-wide from day one.

The operations team needed a way to prove the model's value on a small, contained scope before asking for a larger budget commitment.

The Solution

A phased rollout, starting with one conveyor

Synchronix proposed starting with a single material-handling conveyor — a lower-risk asset with a well-understood failure mode — as a three-month pilot before any conversation about scaling further.

  • → Pilot conveyor mirrored and validated against real maintenance logs for 90 days
  • → Pilot results used to build the business case for a phased, line-by-line expansion
  • → Each new asset type (press, welder, cutter) modeled individually as rollout progressed

"We didn't have to bet the whole budget on a theory. We bet it on one conveyor, saw it work, and scaled from there."

Director of Operations — Steel Fabrication Plant, Southern US
The Results

From one pilot line to fourteen mirrored assets

The pilot conveyor's early-failure detection paid for itself within the first quarter, giving leadership the confidence to approve a phased expansion across cutting, bending, and welding stations. Eight months after the pilot began, 14 assets were running live on the platform.
